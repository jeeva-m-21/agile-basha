# Sanskrit Learning Platform — Product Specification (User Perspective)

**Version:** 1.0 (learner-facing scope)
**Initial learner languages:** English and Tamil
**Scope of this document:** What the learner sees, does, and experiences, and how the platform behaves in response. Technology and architecture are intentionally excluded.

---

## 1. The Promise

> **"Learn to read, understand, and speak simple Sanskrit step by step, in the language you already think in."**

A learner opens the platform knowing little or no Sanskrit. After consistent use, they can:

1. Read Devanāgarī (and, if they choose, Sanskrit written in Tamil script or Roman letters).
2. Pronounce Sanskrit correctly.
3. Understand and build simple sentences.
4. Recognize the grammar behind any word they meet.
5. Read real texts (subhāṣitas, Bhagavad Gītā verses, stotras) with help, and eventually without it.

It is a **learning platform first**. Translation and analysis tools exist to support learning, not to replace it.

---

## 2. Who It Is For

### 2.1 Primary learners

| Learner | Starting point | What they want | What the platform must do for them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tamil-speaking beginner** | Reads Tamil; may know some Grantha letters or recite stotras/mantras by ear; little or no Devanāgarī | To understand what they chant; to read texts in the original | Teach through Tamil, offer Tamil-script reading as a bridge, and use Tamil grammar concepts they already know |
| **English-speaking beginner** | No Indian-script literacy; may have studied yoga/philosophy | To read the Gītā/Upaniṣads, learn the language properly | Teach through English, build script and sounds from zero, explain grammar without assuming Indian-language background |
| **Returning learner** | Studied Sanskrit at school or in a course and forgot it | A structured refresher | Short placement test, skip what they know |
| **Chanter / reciter** | Knows verses by heart, not meanings | Meaning behind what they recite | Reading mode where they enter a verse and understand it word by word |

### 2.2 Not in the first release

Teachers building classes, scholars doing textual research, and children's courses. (See §17.)

## 3. Design Principles (what the learner should feel)

1. **Start small, always know what's next.** One clear next action on the home screen. No menu overload.
2. **Your language, your script.** Explanations in English or Tamil; Sanskrit shown in the script you choose.
3. **Never hide the real Sanskrit.** Transliteration and translation help you read; the original Devanāgarī is always visible and always the goal.
4. **Understand, not memorize.** Every answer gets an explanation, not just "correct/incorrect."
5. **Honest about uncertainty.** Sanskrit is often ambiguous. When a word or sentence can be read more than one way, the platform shows the options instead of picking silently.
6. **Short sessions work.** A meaningful lesson fits in 10–15 minutes, and a single review can be done in 3.
7. **Trust.** Course content is human-written and human-reviewed. Anything generated by AI is clearly labelled.

---

## 4. Language and Script Model

The learner makes **three independent choices**, set during onboarding and changeable any time in Settings.

| Choice | Options (v1) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn in (interface + explanations)** | English · தமிழ் (Tamil) | The language of menus, instructions, lessons, grammar explanations, and feedback |
| **Show Sanskrit in** | Devanāgarī · Tamil script (with Grantha letters) · Roman (IAST) | The script used for Sanskrit examples |
| **Helper line under Sanskrit** | Off · Roman transliteration · Tamil-script transliteration | An optional second line that helps read the original |

**Typical settings**

- Tamil beginner: Learn in தமிழ், Show Sanskrit in Tamil script, helper line on. Over time the platform nudges toward Devanāgarī (see §7.1).
- English beginner: Learn in English, Show Sanskrit in Devanāgarī, helper line Roman (IAST).

**Rules**

- Changing any of these choices changes the **entire platform instantly**, including the lesson the user is in, without losing progress.
- Progress is stored in terms of *what the learner knows* (e.g., "knows the instrumental case"), not in a language, so switching language never resets anything.
- Tamil-script Sanskrit is explicitly marked as a bridge: some Sanskrit sounds cannot be written precisely in Tamil script, and the platform says so where it matters (e.g., distinguishing *ka / kha / ga / gha*), with the Grantha-letter and numeral-subscript conventions explained in a short dedicated lesson.

## 5. The Learner Journey

### 5.1 First visit (no account needed)

1. **Landing page:** one sentence promise, two buttons: **"Start learning"** and **"Try reading a verse."**
2. **Try it now (no sign-up):** the user can try a taster lesson ("Your first five Sanskrit sounds") or paste a Sanskrit line into Reader (§10) and see a basic breakdown. This gives value before asking for anything.
3. After the taster, a gentle prompt: *"Create a free account to save your progress."*

### 5.2 Onboarding (under 2 minutes)

1. Choose **"Learn in"** language (English / தமிழ்).
2. Choose **script for Sanskrit**.
3. **"What's your goal?"** (pick one or more): Understand mantras/stotras I recite · Read the Bhagavad Gītā · Read classical literature · Learn grammar properly · General interest.
4. **"What's your level?"**
   - *Complete beginner* → starts at Level 0.
   - *I know some* → a **6–8 minute placement quiz** (see §5.3).
5. **Daily time:** 5 / 10 / 20 minutes. Used to size lessons and reminders.
6. Land on **Home** with exactly one highlighted action: *"Start Lesson 1."*

### 5.3 Placement quiz

- Adaptive: starts mid-level and moves up or down based on answers.
- Covers script reading, vocabulary, case identification, simple translation.
- Result screen: *"We suggest starting at Level 2, Lesson 4"* plus a list of topics it believes the learner already knows, which the learner can edit.
- The learner can always override ("Start from the beginning instead").

### 5.4 Daily rhythm

```
Open app → Home shows "Today" →
   1. Review (3–5 min): words and rules due for revision
   2. New lesson (8–12 min)
   3. Read (optional, 2–5 min): a short verse or sentence at your level
→ Session summary → streak updated → tomorrow's preview
```

The learner may do any part alone. There's never a forced order.

### 5.5 Long-term path

Level 0 → Level 5 (see §6), with a **Reading Path** running alongside from Level 1 so learners meet real Sanskrit early.

## 6. Curriculum (what is taught, in order)

Each level has a clear outcome, shown to the learner as a **"You will be able to…"** statement and re-shown as a **"You can now…"** achievement on completion.

### Level 0 — Sounds and Script *(≈ 2 weeks)*

**You will be able to:** read and pronounce every Devanāgarī letter and simple syllables.

- Vowels (short/long), consonant groups (varga), the five places of articulation.
- Conjunct consonants, matras, anusvāra, visarga, virāma.
- Reading drills: words, then short phrases.
- **Pronunciation focus** (differs by learner background; see §9):
  - Tamil learners: aspirated and voiced consonants (*kha, gha, bha*), sibilants (ś, ṣ, s), visarga.
  - English learners: retroflex vs dental (*ṭ/t*), vowel length, ṛ, aspirates.

### Level 1 — First Words and Sentences *(≈ 3–4 weeks)*

**You will be able to:** understand and say simple sentences like *"Rāma goes to the forest."*

- Basic vocabulary (≈ 150 words): people, nature, actions, family, everyday items.
- First-case (nominative) and second-case (accusative) forms for common noun types.
- Present-tense verbs (third person singular and plural), then first and second person.
- Sentence order and the idea that word order is flexible because endings carry meaning.

### Level 2 — Cases (Vibhakti) *(≈ 5–6 weeks)*

**You will be able to:** tell who does what, to whom, with what, and where, from word endings.

- Cases 1–7 plus vocative: meaning, usage, and endings for a-stem masculine/neuter, ā-stem feminine, then i/u-stems.
- Pronouns (*saḥ, sā, tat; aham, tvam*, etc.).
- Postposition-like usage and common prepositions/indeclinables.
- **Tamil Bridge:** Tamil has a parallel traditional case system (வேற்றுமை) with the same numbering. Tamil learners see side-by-side mapping (see §7.3).

### Level 3 — Verbs *(≈ 6–8 weeks)*

**You will be able to:** recognize and form the common verb forms.

- Roots (dhātu) and the idea of a root + ending.
- Present, past (imperfect), future, imperative, optative in the most common classes.
- Parasmaipada / Ātmanepada introduced gently.
- Participles, infinitive, absolutive (*-tvā*, *-ya*).
- Reading passages from simple stories (Pañcatantra-style) and subhāṣitas.

### Level 4 — Sandhi and Compounds *(≈ 6–8 weeks)*

**You will be able to:** split and read joined Sanskrit and understand compounds.

- Vowel sandhi, visarga sandhi, consonant sandhi, with a "sandhi lab" (§8.4).
- Compound types (tatpuruṣa, karmadhāraya, dvandva, bahuvrīhi, etc.) taught through examples, not lists.
- Reading longer verses with sandhi and compounds.

### Level 5 — Reading Real Texts *(ongoing)*

**You will be able to:** read selected classical texts with a dictionary and a little help.

- Guided units: Bhagavad Gītā (chosen chapters), selected Upaniṣad passages, Subhāṣitas, short kāvya extracts, common stotras.
- Each unit teaches the specific vocabulary and constructions in that text.

### Reading Path (parallel from Level 1)

Short curated passages unlocked as skills are learned. At Level 1 these are single sentences; at Level 3, short stories and verses; at Level 5, chapters.

## 7. Lessons — What a Learner Experiences

### 7.1 Anatomy of a lesson (≈ 10–15 min)

Every lesson follows the same shape so the learner is never lost.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1. Goal** | One sentence: *"Today: say who does what using the nominative and accusative."* |
| **2. See it** | A real example first. E.g., **रामः वनं गच्छति** with audio, helper line, and translation in the learner's language. |
| **3. Notice it** | Interactive highlighting: tap *रामः* and see *"Rāma, the doer."* Tap *-ं* and see *"marks the thing being gone to."* The learner discovers the pattern before reading the rule. |
| **4. Rule in plain words** | Short explanation in the learner's language, with a Sanskrit term introduced beside it (e.g., "doer — *kartā*, nominative — *prathamā*"). |
| **5. Try it** | 5–8 quick exercises (see §8). Immediate feedback. |
| **6. Say it** | Optional speaking/repeating step with audio models (see §9). |
| **7. Recap** | "You learned…" with 3 bullet points and new words added to the learner's review list. |

### 7.2 Script transition for Tamil-script learners

If the learner shows Sanskrit in Tamil script, the platform gradually introduces Devanāgarī:

- Level 0: both scripts side by side for each letter.
- Level 1: example sentences show Devanāgarī primary with Tamil-script helper.
- Level 2+: the platform *suggests* switching primary display to Devanāgarī (*"You've learned all the letters. Try reading with Devanāgarī as the main script?"*). It never forces it.

### 7.3 The Tamil Bridge (a defining feature)

When a lesson is taught in Tamil, relevant points connect to what a Tamil speaker already knows:

- **Cases:** side-by-side table of Sanskrit vibhakti 1–7 and 8th (vocative) with Tamil வேற்றுமை (first: பெயர், second: ஐ, third: ஆல், fourth: கு, fifth: இன், sixth: அது, seventh: கண், eighth: விளி). The learner sees *"Sanskrit second case ↔ Tamil ஐ"* with an example in both languages.
- **Shared vocabulary:** words familiar in Tamil (e.g., many everyday and religious terms) are flagged: *"You already know this word."*
- **Tamil-friendly contrasts:** short notes where Sanskrit works differently (e.g., Sanskrit has distinct aspirated/voiced consonants Tamil script does not mark; Sanskrit has three numbers: singular, dual, plural, while Tamil uses singular and plural).
- **Same example, two languages:**
  *रामः वनं गच्छति* → English: *Rāma goes to the forest.* → Tamil: *ராமன் காட்டிற்குச் செல்கிறான்.*

A parallel **English Bridge** gives English-speaking learners comparisons to English structures (subject/object, "to/with/by/from" prepositions and how Sanskrit uses endings instead), and to Latin/Greek-style case systems if the learner has seen them.

### 7.4 Lesson controls

- Pause and resume anywhere; resume exactly where left off, across devices.
- **"I need this explained differently"** button: gets another explanation of the same idea (different example, simpler wording, or more detailed).
- **"Show me the Sanskrit rule"** reveals the traditional terminology/rule for those who want depth (progressive disclosure).
- Change difficulty of explanation: **Simple · Standard · Detailed.**

## 8. Practice and Feedback

### 8.1 Exercise types (v1)

| Exercise | Example |
|---|---|
| Read the script | Show a word in Devanāgarī → pick the correct reading |
| Listen and choose | Hear a word → pick what was said |
| Match | Match Sanskrit words to meanings |
| Fill the blank | *रामः ___ गच्छति* → choose the correct form |
| Identify the case | Tap the word in the instrumental case |
| Build the sentence | Arrange word tiles to form a sentence |
| Translate (to / from Sanskrit) | Tile-based or typed, with flexible acceptance of valid word orders |
| Transliterate | Convert Devanāgarī ↔ Roman/Tamil script |
| Split the sandhi | Divide a joined phrase into its words |
| Recall | Type or select the meaning from memory |

### 8.2 Feedback rules

Every answer produces feedback with three parts:

1. **Right or not yet:** supportive tone, never punishing.
2. **Why:** a one-or-two-sentence explanation in the learner's language.
3. **What to do:** a link to the exact lesson/rule, and the item goes back into the review queue if missed.

Example (wrong answer on instrumental case):
> *Not quite. "रामेण" ends in **-ेण**, the instrumental ("by/with"). "रामः" is the doer, so it is the nominative. Review: Instrumental case, Lesson 2.4.*

- Typed answers: the platform accepts reasonable equivalent answers (valid word orders, alternate spellings across scripts) and says so (*"Also correct: …"*).
- For genuinely ambiguous prompts, it accepts all valid readings.

### 8.3 Review (spaced repetition)

- Every word, form, and rule the learner meets enters a **review schedule**.
- "Review" on Home shows how many items are due. Reviews are short, mixed, and adapt: items the learner misses return sooner.
- Learner can mark an item "I know this" or "Reset."

### 8.4 Sandhi Lab (Level 4)

An interactive tool: pick or type two words, see them combine, with each step highlighted and the rule named in plain language. The reverse mode takes a joined phrase and asks the learner to split it, then shows the correct split(s).

## 9. Pronunciation and Audio

### 9.1 What the learner gets

- **Recorded human audio** by qualified Sanskrit speakers for every lesson word, sentence, and verse. (Not synthetic speech in v1; correct vowel length, visarga, and aspirates matter too much.)
- **Slow and normal speed** toggle.
- **Syllable view:** a word broken into syllables with long/short marking (*rā-maḥ*, *gac-cha-ti*), highlighted in time with the audio.
- **Mouth-place guide:** for tricky sounds, a short visual (*"ṭ — tongue tip curled back"*), tuned to the learner's background (Tamil and English learners get different warnings; see Level 0).

### 9.2 Speaking practice (optional, v1 light)

- **Listen → repeat → compare:** the learner records themselves and plays it beside the model audio. No automated scoring in v1, since reliable Sanskrit speech scoring is not yet established. The platform says this plainly.
- Chanting mode for verses: line-by-line audio with text highlighting, and a loop function.

## 10. Reader — Tap-to-Understand Reading

The Reader is where learning meets real Sanskrit.

### 10.1 Using it

1. Paste or type Sanskrit (Devanāgarī, Tamil script, or Roman — auto-detected), or pick a text from the library.
2. The platform shows:
   - The text in the learner's chosen script, with helper line.
   - **Translation** in English or Tamil, labelled as **"AI-assisted translation"** unless it is from a human-reviewed library text.
   - **Word-by-word view:** tap any word for its meaning, root/base word, and grammar ("instrumental singular of *rāma*").
   - **Sandhi split** shown when words are joined.
3. **Progressive disclosure:**
   Simple meaning → word meanings → grammar → Sanskrit rule → sources.

### 10.2 Honest ambiguity

When a word or segmentation has more than one possible reading, the Reader shows **"This can be read more than one way"** and lists the options with brief context. It does not pretend certainty. Labels: **Clear · Possible · Ambiguous.**

### 10.3 Learning from reading

- **"Save word"** adds it to the learner's vocabulary and review.
- **"Practice this sentence"** generates quick exercises from the text the learner just read.
- Words the learner already knows from lessons are shown in a subtle highlight; new words in another. The learner sees how much of the text they already understand (*"You know 18 of 24 words here"*).

### 10.4 Text library (v1 — small and curated)

- Bhagavad Gītā (selected chapters), selected subhāṣitas, common stotras and short Upaniṣad passages, short graded stories.
- Each text shows: source, original Sanskrit, translation (with the translator named, or marked AI-assisted), and difficulty level.
- Only texts with confirmed rights to publish are included.

### 10.5 Interpretive texts

For religious/philosophical texts the platform presents **literal meaning** and notes that traditional commentaries differ. It does not claim one interpretation is definitive. Named commentaries are added only when licensed.

## 11. Dictionary and Grammar Reference

### 11.1 Dictionary

- Search by Sanskrit (any script) or by English/Tamil meaning. Searching *"shiva"* or *"சிவ"* finds **शिव**.
- Results show: Devanāgarī, transliteration, meaning(s) in the learner's language, word type, root where relevant, example sentences, and the dictionary source.
- **Two clearly separated tabs:**
  - **Dictionary:** what the word means (headword/base form).
  - **Form analysis:** what this specific form is (e.g., "*gacchati*: present tense, third person singular of root *gam*").
- Words link back to the lesson where they were taught.

### 11.2 Grammar reference

- Searchable pages for each concept: cases, verb tenses, sandhi, compounds, etc.
- Each page: plain explanation, table, 3–5 examples with audio, and a "Practice this" button.
- Language-matched (English or Tamil) with the Sanskrit technical terms alongside.

## 12. Ask the Tutor (AI helper)

A chat panel available in lessons, Reader, and Dictionary.

### 12.1 What it does

- Answers questions in the learner's language: *"Why is it रामेण here?"* / *"இந்த வார்த்தையின் வேர் என்ன?"* (*"What is the root of this word?"*).
- Is **context-aware:** it knows which lesson, sentence, or word the learner is looking at.
- Offers examples, rewords explanations, and creates extra practice on request (*"Quiz me on the instrumental case."*).

### 12.2 What it must never do

- Invent grammar facts. Grammatical statements are grounded in the platform's verified analysis and lesson content; if it doesn't know, it says so.
- Present AI interpretation of scripture as the one correct meaning.
- Replace the curriculum. It supports lessons; it isn't the course.

### 12.3 What the learner sees

- Answers labelled **"AI tutor"** with a **"Report a problem"** button on every message.
- Links to the relevant lesson/grammar page.
- A daily fair-use limit shown in a friendly way (*"You have 12 questions left today"*).

## 13. Progress, Motivation, and Habit

### 13.1 What progress looks like

- **Skill map:** a visual map of topics (script, cases, verbs, sandhi, etc.) with each node showing *Not started · Learning · Strong · Needs review*.
- **Level progress:** bar with "You can now…" statements that unlock as lessons are completed.
- **Vocabulary count** and **words you can read** in the Reader.
- **Reading milestones:** *"You read your first complete verse."*

### 13.2 Motivation (restrained)

- Daily streak with **a forgiving rule** (one "rest day" per week doesn't break it).
- Gentle reminder at the learner-chosen time (optional).
- Completion celebrations that are short and respectful of the subject. No childish gimmicks, no competitive leaderboards in v1.
- **Weekly summary:** time spent, new words, strongest area, one suggested focus.

### 13.3 Never

- No dark patterns: no guilt-based notifications, no hidden countdowns.
- No locking basic learning behind streak anxiety.

## 14. Screens and Navigation

### 14.1 Bottom/top navigation (five items)

| Tab | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Today's plan: Review, next lesson, optional reading; streak and goal |
| **Learn** | The course map: levels, units, lessons, skill map |
| **Read** | Reader and text library |
| **Practice** | Review queue, free practice by topic, sandhi lab |
| **Me** | Saved words, progress, settings (language, script, daily goal), account |

Dictionary and Ask the Tutor are available from a persistent **search/ask bar** and within every lesson/reading screen rather than as separate tabs.

### 14.2 Home (the most important screen)

- Large primary button: **Continue: Level 2 · Lesson 4, Instrumental case (10 min)**
- Review card: **"12 items due (≈4 min)"**
- Reading card: **"Today's verse: a subhāṣita you can now read"**
- Small streak and weekly goal indicator.

### 14.3 Lesson screen

Single column, one task at a time, big Sanskrit text, audio button always visible, progress dots at the top, "explain differently" and "Ask tutor" at the bottom.

### 14.4 Key empty/edge states

- First day with no review items → Review card says "Your first reviews appear tomorrow."
- Offline → current lesson (downloaded) still usable; other parts show a clear offline message. (Lessons for the next 7 days download in the background where supported.)
- Result unavailable (e.g., AI tutor down) → the platform still works; only the tutor/translation features show a friendly notice.

## 15. Account, Data, and Privacy (learner's view)

- **Optional account:** the taster and Reader work without sign-up; progress is saved to an account (email, Google).
- Learners can **export their data** (words, progress) and **delete their account** from Settings.
- Clear note wherever text is sent for AI processing (*"Your pasted text is processed by an AI service to produce this translation."*).
- No selling of data. Reader history is stored only if the learner opts in.
- Parental consent flow before launch for under-18 sign-ups, per local law.

---

## 16. Accessibility and Devices

- Works on **phone, tablet, and desktop** from the first release; phone is the primary design target.
- Sanskrit always rendered as **text** (never an image), with good Devanāgarī and Tamil fonts, and adjustable text size.
- Keyboard navigation, screen-reader support with correct language tagging for Sanskrit, Tamil, and English.
- High-contrast mode; motion can be reduced; audio always has a text equivalent.
- Typing Sanskrit: built-in on-screen helper for typing Devanāgarī/IAST for users without those keyboards (including typing in Roman and having it convert).

## 18. Content Standards

1. **Human-authored curriculum.** Every lesson written or adapted by qualified Sanskrit teachers and reviewed by at least one more, in both English and Tamil. Tamil is **authored for Tamil learners**, not machine-translated from English.
2. **Terminology consistency.** One approved glossary for grammar terms in English, Tamil, and Sanskrit.
3. **Accuracy review.** Reviewers can flag errors; learners can report problems on any lesson, exercise, or answer; flagged items are triaged within a defined time.
4. **Labelling.** Every piece of content shows its nature: *Course content · Library text (source) · AI-assisted translation · AI tutor.*
5. **Audio.** Recorded by speakers vetted for correct pronunciation, with consistent style.
6. **Rights.** Only content with documented permission or public-domain status is published, with attribution displayed.

## 19. How We Know It Works (learner-centered measures)

| Question | Measure |
|---|---|
| Do learners get started? | % who complete the first lesson on day 1 |
| Do they come back? | Returning within 7 days; 4-week retention |
| Are they learning? | Review accuracy over time; placement-to-end-of-level test improvement; % who can read a new sentence without help |
| Can they read? | Number of Reader texts completed; "words known" share in texts they open |
| Is the content trustworthy? | Rate of learner-reported errors per 1,000 items; reviewer pass rate |
| Do both language groups succeed? | All the above, split by English vs Tamil, so neither is under-served |
| Do they like it? | In-app rating after lessons; short monthly survey |

## 20. Open Questions to Decide Before Building

1. **Pedagogical approach:** grammar-first (rules then practice) vs. reading-first (example then rule)? This spec assumes example-first, then rule; confirm with teachers.
2. **Tamil-script Sanskrit conventions:** which Grantha/subscript-numeral convention is the standard the platform teaches, and how strictly?
3. **Who writes the course?** Which teachers/institutions, and what review process?
4. **Audio production:** who records, and in what accent/tradition?
5. **Business model:** free, free-with-limits, or paid? What stays free (suggested: the full beginner path and Reader basics)?
6. **Which texts first,** and which translations have rights?
7. **Exam/credential ambitions?** Will learners expect a certificate or alignment with existing exams?
8. **Community:** is a discussion space wanted in v1, and who moderates it?
